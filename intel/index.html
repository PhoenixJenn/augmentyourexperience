AI Spending vs. Productivity Reality 5/7 briefs

2026-05-31: Goldman Sachs 24x token demand projection added a hard quantitative anchor to the capex story this week — moving it from anecdote to structural trajectory. Two sides of the same bet. On the spending side: Meta's $145–200B AI buildout funded by 8,000 job cuts, tokenmaxxing driving cost crises at Microsoft/Meta/Amazon, OpenAI losing $1.25 per $1 revenue pre-IPO, Goldman projecting 24x token demand growth. On the returns side: productivity gains are still unproven. The tell is which companies quietly rehire 6–12 months after AI-justified cuts vs. those that actually extract the efficiency. CEO survey data is directional noise — actual headcount actions and earnings commentary are the signal. The 4-quarter reckoning starts now.

Cybersecurity 1/7 briefs

AI is both expanding the attack surface and becoming a target. Nation-state OT attacks on critical infrastructure (LA Metro/Iran), prompt injection emerging as supply chain warfare (jqwik), Python AI agent ecosystem vulns (Starlette ASGI), ECB writing regulatory expectations for AI-led attacks, IoT botnets at 17M-device scale, and phishing targeting AI recovery keys. The common thread: security teams are under-resourced for the speed at which AI is creating new vectors.

Autonomous Coding Agent Economics 1/7 briefs

Cognition (Devin) at $25B pre-money / $492M ARR is the extreme case for the autonomous coding agent thesis. GitHub Copilot billing backlash is the demand destruction signal. The question is whether enterprise is paying for proof or promise — and whether 50x ARR multiples hold when the tools start competing on price.

AI Infrastructure Backlash 1/7 briefs

The physical and political costs of AI scaling are generating organized resistance. Governor-level executive orders on data center siting (Utah/Stratos), community opposition framed through environmental justice (Erin Brockovich data center map), and Goldman Sachs projecting 24x token demand growth as the forcing function behind the buildout. Watch whether other states follow Utah and whether the backlash becomes a permitting bottleneck.

3D Printing & Digital Manufacturing Policy 1/7 briefs

California's 3D printer gun-blocking software bill is the first major legislation treating consumer fabrication hardware as a regulated device — forcing a decision about who controls firmware on printers you own. Bambu Lab's existing community fight (proprietary DRM lockout of third-party filament) turns out to be a preview of this dynamic. The question: does regulation accelerate hardware DRM consolidation (Bambu wins) or create an opening for open-platform alternatives (Prusa, Creality, Voron)? This is right-to-repair applied to digital manufacturing. Watch for other states, EU maker-rights responses, and whether Bambu uses compliance as a moat.

Maker Rights & Consumer Hardware DRM 1

The California 3D printer gun-blocking bill is the first legislation treating consumer fabrication hardware as regulated software. Bambu Lab's proprietary lockout was a preview — now it's a policy template. Watch whether right-to-repair frameworks extend to digital manufacturing.
