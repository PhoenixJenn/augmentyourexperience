Week in Review

May 18 – May 24, 2026 · Morning Brief Weekly Digest · Weekly Brief
This Week's Top Stories
  • Musk v. OpenAI — over on procedure: After three weeks of testimony from Musk, Altman, and Nadella, a nine-person jury deliberated fewer than two hours and ruled unanimously that Musk's 2024 lawsuit was barred by the statute of limitations — the jury found he knew of OpenAI's restructuring plans as early as 2021. No ruling on the merits. The verdict clears OpenAI's corporate restructuring and substantially de-risks a near-term IPO, potentially as soon as September.
  • Anthropic hits $10.9B quarterly revenue, turns profitable: Anthropic told investors it expects Q2 revenue to more than double to ~$10.9 billion — a ~$43B annualized run rate — and its first profitable quarter. The company is simultaneously paying xAI $1.25 billion a month to rent compute capacity from the Memphis Colossus 1 cluster, and acquired SDK-automation startup Stainless, whose customer list includes OpenAI and Google.
  • Google I/O goes all-in on agents and smart glasses: Google launched Gemini 3.5 Flash, Gemini Omni, Spark (its autonomous coding agent), and Android XR smart glasses in partnership with Samsung, Warby Parker, and Gentle Monster — with audio-only glasses shipping fall 2026 and display-equipped glasses deferred to 2027. XREAL's Project Aura (Android XR, display-equipped) will ship before end of 2026. Google also integrated Street View into its Genie world model, now available to AI Ultra subscribers globally.
  • SpaceX files for what could be the largest IPO in history: The S-1 targets a ~$1.75 trillion valuation on a Nasdaq listing as early as June 12 under ticker SPCX. 2025 revenue was $18.67B (+33% YoY), Starlink alone contributed $11B+, but the company lost $4.9B and nearly doubled capex to $20.7B. xAI burned $6.4B last year; Grok downloads collapsed from 20M in January to 8.3M in April.
  • Smart glasses summer is officially on: In a four-month window: Google (I/O, May), Snap consumer Specs at ~$2,500 (fall), Samsung Galaxy Glasses at Galaxy Unpacked July 22 in London, and Meta Connect September 23–24 with new glasses teased. LetinAR, the LG-backed South Korean optics startup, raised $18.5M ahead of a 2027 IPO — a supply-chain signal that the form-factor bottleneck is finally breaking.
  • AI capex is being funded by layoffs — at scale: Meta cut ~8,000 employees (10% of staff) and canceled 6,000 open roles, explicitly to offset its $145B+ AI infrastructure spend, including the $200B Hyperion data center in Louisiana. Intuit cut 3,000 jobs (17% of workforce) for the same reason. Statista projects 2026 tech layoffs will outpace both 2024 and 2025. Standard Chartered separately announced 7,800+ back-office cuts by 2030, replacing them with AI.
AI & Industry

OpenAI — enterprise, sovereign, and IPO track — OpenAI opened its first applied AI lab outside the US in Singapore with a $235M commitment and ~200 staff, aligned to Singapore's public-sector AI priorities. Separately, it signed a national deal giving all Maltese citizens ChatGPT Plus. On the enterprise side: a Dell partnership brings Codex to hybrid/on-prem environments for regulated industries; GPT-5.5 landed in Databricks agent workflows, setting a new state of the art on OfficeQA Pro; and a new personal finance experience for US Pro users lets subscribers connect financial accounts for AI-guided advice. With the Musk lawsuit cleared, an IPO is reportedly targeting September.

Anthropic — distribution moats over raw compute — Beyond the revenue milestone, Anthropic's week illustrated a clear strategic posture: own the developer layer. Its acquisition of Stainless gives it the tooling that builds and maintains SDKs for OpenAI, Google, and Cloudflare — an infrastructure play with obvious competitive visibility. SandboxAQ (Alphabet spinoff) also brought its drug-discovery models into Claude, betting that access — not model quality — is the bottleneck in computational chemistry. Separately, Anthropic's Project Glasswing (Claude Mythos) uncovered 10,000+ high/critical vulnerability candidates across systemically important software in one month; 1,726 validated as true positives. Defensive AI discovery is now outpacing human patch capacity.

Google I/O — AI in everything, agents everywhere — Gemini 3.5 Flash is Google's new flagship coding and agentic model. Gemini Omni is a multimodal family (text/image/audio/video in, video out) with SynthID watermarking on by default. Spark is Google's autonomous coding agent (Claude Code / Codex territory), and Antigravity 2.0 is the agent platform. Search gets a dynamic expanding box, AI Overviews, and background Information Agents. Gmail adds conversational voice search. YouTube gets "Ask YouTube" (Premium-gated in the US) and Gemini Omni for Shorts. Google Docs can now organize a spoken first draft. The ten-blue-links era is functionally over.

AI governance, safety, and trust — a rough week — Trump pulled an AI executive order within hours of signing it, citing concern it would slow America's lead over China; the US now has no pre-deployment security review framework for frontier models. OpenAI and Anthropic are lobbying on opposite sides of AI policy in Washington. OpenAI joined the C2PA standard and adopted Google's SynthID watermarking (100B images/videos labeled to date). University of Florida researchers found false-negative rates as high as 99.6% in the five most popular AI text detectors. Nearly 150,000 fake citations from AI hallucinations have appeared in published research. A Granta-published Commonwealth Short Story Prize regional winner appeared to be AI-written; a book about AI distorting truth contained synthetic quotes attributed to real people including Kara Swisher.

AI economics — the cost crisis arrives — OpenAI reportedly loses $1.25 for every dollar in revenue. Microsoft, Meta, and Amazon are hitting AI cost crises from employee "tokenmaxxing" — agentic workflows consuming up to 1,000× more tokens than standard queries. Anthropic's Claude Code event in London found ~half the developers present had shipped pull requests entirely written by Claude without reading the code first. DeepSeek made its 75% V4 Pro price cut permanent: $0.003–$0.87 per million tokens vs. GPT-5's $2.50 input price — a 50–100× gap that enterprise procurement is noticing. Viktor, an AI coworker for Slack/Teams, hit $15M ARR in 10 weeks on a $75M Series A from Accel.

Chip geopolitics — full bifurcation underway — Taiwan arrested three individuals in its first semiconductor-smuggling crackdown, allegedly diverting Nvidia Hopper servers to China via forged documents. China blocked Nvidia's RTX 5090D V2 — while Jensen Huang was in Beijing. AMD's Lisa Su met China's Vice Premier He Lifeng and announced $10B+ in Taiwan ecosystem investments. Alibaba's T-Head unveiled Zhenwu M890 GPU specs, claiming scaled mass production. The Trump administration is moving $100B+ of EXIM lending capacity toward US AI export packages. Nvidia disclosed $43B in startup holdings and flagged a new $200B CPU market for AI agent orchestration.

Spatial Computing & XR

Android XR — Google's glasses strategy lands — Google and Samsung previewed audio-only Android XR smart glasses launching fall 2026, with Warby Parker and Gentle Monster collections at launch and Gucci-branded glasses in 2027. Sameer Samat explicitly cited Google Glass's failure as a "fashion first" lesson. XREAL's Project Aura — the first display-equipped Android XR glasses — confirmed a pre-end-of-2026 ship date, with a tethered compute puck that doubles as a controller. Android XR also shipped its first major platform update: auto-spatialization for 2D content, hand occlusion, and window-wall pinning. One analyst projects ~2M Google glasses units in 2026, ahead of Meta Ray-Ban's first-year pace.

Snap Specs and the consumer AR glasses moment — Veteran journalist Alex Heath reported Snap's consumer Specs (true AR with display) will launch this fall priced at approximately $2,500 — developer/enthusiast pricing, not mass market. Snap CEO Evan Spiegel is keynoting AWE USA in June for the second consecutive year, ahead of launch. Snap's subscription product is at a $1B run rate. At $2,500 Specs will be the first display-equipped AR glasses in serious consumer distribution since HoloLens, and will set price expectations for competing launches.

Samsung Galaxy Glasses and Meta Connect — Samsung is reportedly unveiling its first smart glasses at Galaxy Unpacked July 22 in London, alongside the Z Fold 8 and Flip 8. Leaked images show a notably slim display-less variant; a display-equipped SKU exists in One UI 9 code. Meta confirmed Connect 2026 for September 23–24 and teased new smart glasses in the announcement. Meta is also opening Ray-Ban Display glasses to third-party apps in response to Android XR competition. The Anduril–Meta AR military headset, designed for drone-strike ordering via eye-tracking and voice, is progressing; defense funding is accelerating the same optics and compute that feeds Meta's consumer roadmap.

World models get real-world grounding — Google DeepMind integrated Street View into Project Genie, enabling generation of interactive, navigable environments grounded in real streets — with weather variation and time-of-day changes — now available globally to AI Ultra subscribers. The marriage of world models and geospatial imagery is a meaningful step toward embodied AI training that doesn't rely on purely synthetic data. Separately, Gracia's volumetric captures became streamable with no app download required, including mixed reality on Quest 3, with a Vision Pro app coming. True 6DoF moving volumetric video over consumer hardware is a world first for streaming.

Platform and content signals — Meta is winding down Horizon Worlds in VR, pivoting it to mobile/web. Rec Room similarly retreated from VR. VRChat is reporting record usage and explicitly stated it is "not going anywhere" — the community-driven, UGC-chaos model outlasted corporate top-down social VR. Polyarc (Moss) is porting its VR-native franchise to flatscreen this summer following a major project cancellation — a signal about VR-exclusive economics even for beloved titles. Pimax began shipping Dream Air SE after 12+ months of pre-orders; ASUS ROG and XREAL opened pre-orders for ROG XREAL R1 (240Hz micro-OLED, $850). Meta's Horizon OS test channel can now convert 2D browser images into stereoscopic 3D photos using generative depth estimation.

Robotics & AVs

Waymo suspends service in five cities over flooding failures — Waymo suspended robotaxi service in Atlanta, San Antonio, Houston, Dallas, and a fifth city after autonomous vehicles kept driving into flooded streets. A software patch pushed to all 3,791 vehicles two weeks earlier failed to prevent an unoccupied Waymo from getting stuck on a flooded Atlanta street. Waymo also separately paused freeway rides due to construction-zone navigation failures. The pattern — stellar performance on the long tail with persistent failures on non-exotic scenarios like flood recognition — is the defining tension of commercial autonomy deployment. Insurance underwriters and city regulators are watching closely.

Humanoid robotics — capital, demos, and the data layer — Mind Robotics (Rivian spinoff, RJ Scaringe) raised $400M, bringing total to $1B+; Scaringe has now raised $12B+ across three startups. Figure AI's week-long livestream of humanoids handling warehouse packages — including a robot competing against a human intern — became a viral sensation, driving merchandise sales and spawning named fan robots. Barclays estimated China will need up to 24 million deployed humanoids by 2035 to offset a projected 37M-person labor force decline. Korean conglomerates (Samsung, Hyundai, LG) backed Config, a startup positioning itself as the TSMC of robot training data. HyperShell raised an additional $50M (Series B+, co-led by Ant Group and Meituan Dragon Ball) bringing Series B total to $120M at a ~$400M valuation, as it launched its global X-series consumer exoskeleton.

Tesla FSD, Cybercab efficiency, and Robotaxi disclosure — Tesla launched Full Self-Driving Supervised in China, now available in 10 markets. Newly unredacted reports confirmed two Robotaxi crashes involving teleoperators — confirming the service is more human-in-the-loop than marketed. The Cybercab was certified at 165 Wh/mile, 28% more efficient than the Lucid Air Pure (previous record holder), though the comparison is complicated by its purpose-built two-seat, no-pedal design. Separately, 14,575 Model Ys were recalled for a missing federally required weight-certification sticker — missed by an automated vision-scanning tool in the Fremont factory.

AV second-mover thesis and EV market divergence — Nuro argued that being the number-two robotaxi player is an advantage — Waymo absorbed the regulatory and reputational cost of normalization, and Nuro (now pivoted from delivery) has signed deals with Uber and Lucid for tens of thousands of vehicles. Geely-backed eCarX signed a $750M deal to supply purpose-built robotaxis for May Mobility. Global EV sales are surging outside the US; the post-tax-credit US market is going K-shaped. Stellantis CEO said explicitly the company sees "no space in the United States" for Chinese-branded EVs and is exploring Mexican/Canadian production instead.

3D Scanning & Printing

Bambu Lab — open-source conflict and fire safety questions resurface — Bambu Lab privately messaged a developer on Reddit asking him to delete code enabling remote control of Bambu printers without Bambu's software, despite the company's printers being partly built on open-source code. The message leaked and the 3D printing community mobilized, with crowdfunding for a legal response. The same week, Tom's Hardware reported a melted Bambu A1, reigniting debate about whether the unit remains a fire risk following last year's recall. Bambu's brand trust — the category's most significant — is under real pressure from both directions simultaneously.

Consumer printing commoditizes; Gaussian splatting goes consumer — Revopoint launched the POP 4 on Kickstarter, billed as the world's first AI-powered 3D scanner with photoreal 3D Gaussian Splatting export — going from research paper to consumer scanner output format in under three years. On the printer side, Elegoo Centauri Carbon 2 Combo dropped to $399 (down $50), Bambu, Elegoo, and Creality are all running Memorial Day discounts with some models $300 off. The line between AI-generated 3D content and scanned content is blurring: when a unified multimodal model (see: Gemini Omni) can generate a usable 3D asset from a text prompt, whether to scan or generate becomes a workflow decision.

IoT

The IoT product lifecycle problem — three case studies — AcuRite is killing its My AcuRite smart weather app on May 30, forcing migration to AcuRite NOW (which lacks several legacy features). Starbucks pulled its AI-powered inventory management tool nine months into North American deployment after it repeatedly confused milk types — reverting to manual counts, a CEO-visible AI bet that didn't survive real stores. And Google's original Chromecast (2013, 10M units sold in 2014 alone) failed en masse this week — a server-side auth issue, not hardware death, but a reminder that every connected product has an indefinite cloud dependency that someone eventually stops funding.

Biometrics, privacy, and smart home standards — NYC Health and Hospitals disclosed a breach affecting at least 1.8 million people, including biometric data and fingerprints — a reminder that biometrics can't be rotated like passwords. Disney faces a class action over facial recognition in parks. Aqara launched the Camera Hub G350, the first Matter-certified smart camera; Matter 1.5 includes cameras, but Apple Home does not yet support them — Apple's support is expected at WWDC June 8 and would meaningfully erode Ring/Nest/Arlo lock-in. AT&T sued California to stop providing traditional landline service, spending $1B/year on copper infrastructure serving ~3% of households. Helium Mobile is killing its free Zero Plan June 11, moving customers to a $15/month Air Plan.

Media & Entertainment

AI content and the open web — the traffic crisis deepens — Google's AI-forward Search redesign (AI Overviews, background Information Agents) is compressing click-through rates to publisher sites. Google's new agent model completes tasks without sending a click downstream; mid-tier publications are shutting down or cutting staff as a result. The circular problem: AI reduces the economic incentive to produce the content AI was trained on. Amazon's Alexa Plus can now generate full AI podcast episodes on demand using licensed content from 200+ newsrooms (AP, Reuters, Washington Post, Time); Amazon positions licensing as the answer to AI content legitimacy. China produced an average of 470 AI-generated short dramas per day in January alone, with production costs collapsed by orders of magnitude.

Sony reverses PC strategy; raises PlayStation Plus prices — Sony confirmed it is ending PC ports for major single-player PlayStation exclusives, with online games remaining multi-platform. The reversal follows PC ports cannibalizing PS5 sales without the expected platform halo. Simultaneously, Sony raised PlayStation Plus prices: US monthly from $9.99 to $10.99, three-month from $24.99 to $27.99, effective May 20. Sony also faces a class action alleging it is profiting from tariff-related price hikes — part of a growing genre of tariff lawsuits also filed against Nintendo and Amazon. Separately, Sony settled a class action over PlayStation Store game vouchers for $7.85M — paid in PSN credit, not cash.

Music, podcasts, and the AI rights reckoning — Spotify and Universal Music struck a deal allowing Spotify Premium subscribers to generate AI covers and remixes from Universal's catalog, with participating artists receiving a revenue share — the first major label to officially license its catalog for user-generated AI derivative works. Spotify also launched a NotebookLM competitor (desktop AI audio briefings from podcasts) and an ElevenLabs-powered audiobook generation tool that doesn't require Spotify exclusivity. YouTube Shorts now sees 2 billion hours watched on TVs per month. Video podcast interoperability arrived: Spotify adopted Apple's HLS streaming tech, letting creators distribute across both platforms without workflow changes.

Streaming, gaming, and labor — Netflix will air "The Breakfast Club" podcast live daily — its first live daily programming, marking the completion of streaming-as-broadcast convergence. Critterz, the OpenAI-backed animated feature built on a generative AI pipeline, missed its Cannes market window after OpenAI deprecated the underlying Sora model version — the first high-profile creative project orphaned by model deprecation. Communication Workers of America added Magic: The Gathering Arena (Hasbro/Wizards of the Coast) developers to its growing gaming union roster, which already includes workers on Call of Duty, Fallout, Overwatch, Diablo, and World of Warcraft; a remote-work rollback was cited as a primary organizing catalyst.

Big Picture

AI capex is being funded by labor — explicitly. Meta, Intuit, and Standard Chartered all announced mass layoffs this week in language that left no room for euphemism: people are being cut to pay for chips and data centers. At the same time, the revenue is real — Anthropic at a $43B run rate, Nvidia posting another record quarter, OpenAI preparing an IPO. The question for the next four quarters is whether AI productivity gains justify the pace of workforce reduction, or whether the labor cuts are running ahead of demonstrated returns. South Korea's deputy prime minister framed it correctly: this is a preview, not an anomaly.

The smart glasses platform war is finally real. For years, smart glasses have been a punchline. After this week, they're a product category with four major launches in four months (Google I/O → Snap in June → Samsung July 22 → Meta September 23–24), credible optics supply chain infrastructure (LetinAR's $18.5M raise and IPO track), and at least one analyst projecting Google ships 2M units in 2026. The question shifts from "will this ever happen?" to "which platform wins the face?" — and Android XR's multi-OEM model gives Google a structural distribution advantage Meta's walled garden cannot easily replicate.

Agentic AI is outpacing governance everywhere it deploys. Anthropic engineers shipping unread Claude-written code. OpenAI's Codex operating Macs while the screen is locked. Google's Spark launching with autonomous-purchasing disclaimers buried in APK teardowns. Waymo's flood patch failing in production across five cities. These aren't isolated incidents — they share a structure: the capability ships faster than the oversight framework. The companies and enterprise buyers that build confirmation friction, audit trails, and red-team discipline into agentic deployments now will have a significant advantage when the first high-profile incident forces a regulatory response.

The AI price war has arrived from China, and the infrastructure bifurcation is irreversible. DeepSeek's permanent 75% price cut on V4 Pro — at $0.003–$0.87/million tokens versus GPT-5's $2.50 — creates a 50–100× cost gap that enterprise procurement cannot ignore for commodity AI workloads. Meanwhile Taiwan arrested semiconductor smugglers, China blocked Nvidia's 5090D V2, and the US is weaponizing EXIM financing for AI exports. A bifurcated global AI infrastructure is no longer a forecast — it's an operating reality. The companies that pick a side clearly, or that can navigate both stacks without getting caught in the middle, are the ones that will outperform.
